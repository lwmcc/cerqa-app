How to keep multiple cars together on a road trip

November 9, 2026 · 2 min read · ES

Say a group of friends is heading to the beach in San Diego. One car leaves from Los Angeles, another from Orange County. Somewhere on the freeway someone makes a yellow light, the others don't, and now nobody knows who is ahead. Someone needs gas. Someone takes the wrong exit. And the passenger in every car is texting "where are you?"

Traveling in separate cars doesn't have to mean traveling separately. Here's how to keep everyone together, from planning to arrival.

Before you leave

  • Agree on the destination and the stops. Decide where you're going and where you'll stop for gas, food or a break. A stop everyone knows about is a natural place to regroup.
  • Pick a navigator in each car. The driver drives. The passenger handles the phone: checking where the others are, reading messages and answering.
  • Start sharing before anyone pulls out. If everyone is already on the map when you leave, nobody has to stop later to set it up.

Share your location with the whole group

Most phones can share a location, but on a road trip you want something a little different: everyone on one map at the same time, whether they have an iPhone or an Android phone, and an arrival time for each car.

With Cerqa you create a group, tap Share location, pick the group and choose how long to share. When the others accept, each car shows up on the map with how far away it is and when it will get there. Sharing ends by itself when the time is up, and anyone can stop sharing at any time.

Everyone also gets a group chat, so "stopping for gas at the next exit" reaches every car at once instead of going out in three different text threads.

When the group gets split up

It happens on every trip with more than one car. A light, a lane that ends, a truck that won't let you merge. When everyone is sharing, a split is no big deal:

  • Don't chase. You can see where the other car is, so there's no reason to speed or weave to catch up. Catch up when it's safe, or let the car ahead slow down.
  • Regroup at the next stop. If you're far apart, meet at the stop you already agreed on instead of trying to find each other on the freeway.
  • Watch the arrival times. If one car is 10 minutes behind, the others know whether to wait at the beach or grab a table.

Stay safe

Keeping the group together is never worth an unsafe move. Let the passenger handle the phone, don't text and drive, and remember that a missed exit costs a few minutes. Everyone can still see where you are.

Questions

What is the best way to keep multiple cars together on a road trip?

Agree on the destination and stops before you leave, have a passenger in each car handle the phone, and share live locations with the whole group so every car can see the others and their arrival times.
