Tijuana to Playa del Carmen by car

Coast to coast across Mexico: the Sonoran Desert, the Pacific in Mazatlán, the highlands around Guadalajara and Puebla, then the Gulf and the Caribbean.

  • About 2,700 mi4,350 km
  • About 50 hdriving
  • 7 days
  • MexicoBaja California to Quintana Roo

Before you go

  • Passport and a tourist permit (FMM). See INM (Mexico immigration).
  • Vehicle permit (TIP) from Banjercito: you need one as soon as you leave the border zone (in Sonora, the free zone). Get it online 10 to 60 days ahead or at a Banjercito office at the border. It takes a refundable deposit based on the car's year. Cancel it at the border on your way out to get the deposit back.
  • Mexican car insurance. US policies generally don't cover you in Mexico.
  • Pesos for tolls. Most of the way is on toll roads (cuotas). Many booths take cards, but not all, so carry cash.
  • Safety: the route crosses states with different US travel advisory levels, and they change. Read the US travel advisory for Mexico for each state before you go.

The route

  1. Day 1: Tijuana to Hermosillo

    About 550 mi (885 km), 10 h

    • Cross at Otay Mesa, San Ysidro or Tecate. Mexico Highway 2D east climbs La Rumorosa, a steep grade with wind and sometimes fog, to Mexicali.
    • Then Highway 2 through San Luis Río Colorado and the Altar Desert to Sonoyta and Caborca, and south to Hermosillo.
    • Fill up whenever you're at half a tank. Some desert stretches have long gaps between stations.

  2. Day 2: Hermosillo to Mazatlán

    About 535 mi (860 km), 10 h

    • Highway 15, four lanes most of the way: Guaymas, Ciudad Obregón, Navojoa, Los Mochis, Culiacán.
    • Many toll booths today. Keep pesos handy.
    • Carry your vehicle permit. You're outside the free zone now.

  3. Day 3: Mazatlán to Guadalajara

    About 305 mi (490 km), 5.5 h

    • Highway 15D through Tepic and into the mountains. A shorter day, with time for Mazatlán's malecón in the morning.

  4. Day 4: Guadalajara to Puebla

    About 405 mi (650 km), 7.5 h

    • Toll roads to Querétaro, then the Arco Norte around Mexico City, so you skip its traffic and driving restrictions.

  5. Day 5: Puebla to Villahermosa

    About 405 mi (650 km), 7.5 h

    • Highway 150D east past Pico de Orizaba, down the Cumbres de Maltrata grade (often foggy), through Córdoba and on to the Gulf.
    • Detour: some drivers go through Veracruz city or Oaxaca instead. It's slower but has great stops.

  6. Day 6: Villahermosa to Bacalar

    About 335 mi (540 km), 6.5 h

    • Highway 186 east through Escárcega. Long stretches with few towns, so fill up in Escárcega.
    • Bacalar's lagoon of seven colors is worth the night.

  7. Day 7: Bacalar to Playa del Carmen

    About 175 mi (280 km), 3.5 h

    • Highway 307 north through Felipe Carrillo Puerto and Tulum.
    • Quintana Roo is an hour ahead of central Mexico all year.

Good to know

  • Most people driving across Mexico plan to arrive before dark.
  • Military and National Guard checkpoints are routine. Slow down, be polite and have your papers ready.
  • Watch for speed bumps (topes) entering every town, often unmarked.
  • Gas stations are full service. Check that the pump starts at zero. Prices: gas prices in Mexico.
  • Roadside help: the Ángeles Verdes (Green Angels) answer at 078.
  • Two cars? Agree on each day's gas stops and the hotel before you lose signal in the desert.
