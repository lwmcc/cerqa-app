Tijuana Airport to San Diego: CBX or San Ysidro?

November 19, 2026 · 6 min read · ES

Flying out of Tijuana can save a lot of money, and the airport sits right on the border. But the first question everyone asks is the same: what's the easiest way between Tijuana airport and San Diego?

I've done it both ways many times, walking across the Cross Border Xpress (CBX) bridge and driving through the San Ysidro border crossing. Here's what each one is really like, where to park, and how to get picked up on the US side.

The short answer: if you're flying, use CBX. It's quicker, calmer, and it saves you a lot of headaches.

Why Fly Out of Tijuana?

Price. On international trips, flying out of Tijuana can save big compared to flying out of nearby US airports. For our family's international flights, I've found airfare out of Tijuana around 50% cheaper than the same trip from an American airport.

Getting to Tijuana can take some driving. But when you find a discounted fare from TIJ, the savings are worth it, in my opinion. Before you book, compare the fare from Tijuana with your home airport, and count the drive, gas and parking.

How CBX Works

CBX is a covered walkway that connects a terminal in Otay Mesa, San Diego, directly to the Tijuana airport. You cross the border on foot inside the building, without driving through Tijuana at all.

  • Buy your CBX ticket ahead of time. It saves time at the terminal, and CBX says booking early can save you up to 20%.
  • Have your ticket and boarding pass ready. Print them, keep them on your phone, or print them at a kiosk in the terminal. CBX is only for airline passengers, so you need a boarding pass for a flight in or out of Tijuana.
  • Bring your travel documents. A passport for most people. Requirements depend on your citizenship, so check them before you go.
  • Go inside and follow the line. Check-in is quick and easy. Staff direct you through each step.

Not Mexican? You'll need a tourist permit. Visitors entering Mexico need an FMM (Forma Migratoria Múltiple). For longer stays, about a week or more, it comes with a fee called the Derecho de No Residente (non-resident fee), 983 pesos in 2026. Check the INM and CBX sites for how to get it before your trip.

Coming back through CBX

After you land in Tijuana, the way into CBX is inside the baggage claim area. Get your bags, then follow the CBX signs from there. You'll need your CBX ticket, the boarding pass from the flight you just landed on, and your documents for entering the US.

Two things to know: CBX only lets you cross within a short time after you land (2 hours, according to CBX and Volaris), and once you walk out of baggage claim, you can't get back in. If you leave, you'll have to cross another way.

CBX has rules about how close to your flight you can cross in either direction, and they change from time to time. Check the current ones on the official CBX site before your trip.

Coming back into the US, you'll see the live CBX wait on our CBX wait time page, straight from US Customs and Border Protection.

Driving Into Tijuana Instead

You can also drive into Tijuana, through Otay Mesa or San Ysidro, and park at the airport. Either way works, but neither is always easy.

  • Through Otay Mesa, the drive to the airport is short, but traffic can still be heavy. When I drove it, some stretches had poor road conditions. Those may be fixed by now.
  • Through San Ysidro, it's a longer drive across Tijuana, and traffic can be very heavy at times. It can be nerve-wracking if you don't do it often.
  • Parking at Tijuana airport is affordable and good, for short or long stays.
  • You'll see a lot of police. Once, driving out of the airport's long-term lot to head back to the US, I drove right next to federal police in full gear with rifles in the backs of their pickup trucks. That's normal there, but it made me a little nervous.
  • Coming back, the border line is the hard part. Check the Otay Mesa and San Ysidro waits before you leave the airport.

Watch the lane signs early. Years ago, coming back from the airport, we ended up in the SENTRI lane at San Ysidro without SENTRI. We were told to go back around, and it took a long time to drive all the way back and get into the right lane. The lanes split well before the booths, so pick yours as soon as you see the signs.

Both ways work. But if you're flying, CBX saves you the drive through Tijuana and the long line back.

Where to Park

  • At CBX. The easiest: when you come back, you walk straight to your car. Prices have gone up over the years.
  • Offsite with a shuttle. Cheaper, especially for long trips. I've used Lion Parking, which moved to 2739 Cactus Rd in San Diego. You park, and their shuttle takes you to CBX. When you come back, call them and they'll pick you up.
  • On the street nearby. You used to be able to park on nearby streets, but that has mostly ended. I wouldn't count on it.

If I could change one thing about my first trips, I'd have parked offsite for the long stays and saved a few bucks.

Renting a Car at CBX

A one-way rental can save you from leaving your own car at CBX for the whole trip. My wife has done it both ways:

  • Going: she rented a car for the drive on the US side and dropped it off at the Hertz location at CBX.
  • Coming back: she rented a car at CBX and returned it at a rental location near home.

She said it was quick and easy both times, and there's no parking bill waiting when you get back.

Getting Picked Up on the US Side

Getting picked up at CBX is easy if you time it right. The person picking you up can wait at the curb, but not for long. Security will hurry them along, and there's nowhere close to wait on the street anymore.

That means timing is everything. And timing is where it goes wrong.

Once I had to pick my wife up and I was an hour late. She waited with no idea where I was or how long I'd be. If she could have seen where I was, she could have grabbed something to eat instead of standing at the curb wondering.

You can handle this with a phone call, of course. But an app makes it easy to see where the other person is, how far away they are and when they'll get there, without calling someone who's driving.

Tips

  • Buy your CBX ticket before you go. It's faster and usually cheaper.
  • Coming back, don't leave baggage claim. The CBX entrance is in there, and you can't get back in once you walk out.
  • Park offsite for long trips. The shuttle is quick and it adds up to real savings.
  • Driving through San Ysidro? Pick your lane early. Ending up in the wrong one can cost you a long trip back around.
  • Time your pickup. Share your location so the driver arrives when you walk out, not before or an hour after.

Questions

What is the easiest way from Tijuana airport to San Diego?

For airline passengers, Cross Border Xpress (CBX). It's a walkway from the Tijuana airport into a terminal in San Diego, so you cross the border on foot without driving through Tijuana.

Can anyone use CBX?

No. CBX is for airline passengers. You need a CBX ticket, a boarding pass for a flight in or out of Tijuana, and your travel documents. Coming back, you enter CBX from the baggage claim area after you land. Check the current rules on the official CBX site.

Do I have to pay a fee to enter Mexico through CBX?

If you're not Mexican, you need an FMM tourist permit. For longer stays, about a week or more, it comes with the Derecho de No Residente (non-resident fee), 983 pesos in 2026. Check INM and CBX for the current rules.

Is CBX faster than San Ysidro?

For flyers, usually yes. You skip the drive through Tijuana and the car line at San Ysidro. You can see both live waits on cerqa.app.

Where can I park for CBX?

At CBX itself, or at a cheaper offsite lot with a shuttle to the terminal. Street parking nearby has mostly ended.

How do I time a pickup at CBX?

The driver can tap Share a live link in Cerqa while driving to CBX, so the traveler sees exactly when they'll arrive, no app needed. Or the traveler can tap Pick me up, and both see each other on the map until the pickup.
