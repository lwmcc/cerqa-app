How to Make School Pickups Easier With Family Location Sharing

November 16, 2026 · 3 min read · ES

The bell rings. Your daughter grabs her backpack, walks out the front doors, and sends the same text she sends every day:

"Are you here?"

You're stuck behind a line of cars two blocks away. You reply at the next red light: "Almost. Where are you?" She answers: "Front entrance." But the front entrance is crowded, she can't see your car, and you can't see her. A few minutes later: "Which side are you on?"

Meanwhile, your spouse texts too: "Are you getting her, or am I?"

School pickups shouldn't take this many messages. Here's how to make them simpler, for the person waiting and for the person driving.

Why School Pickups Get Complicated

  • Crowded lots and long lines. Dozens of cars arrive at the same time, and they all look alike from the curb.
  • Plans change during the day. Practice runs late, a club meeting gets canceled, or your teen wants a ride from a friend's house instead.
  • Two parents, one pickup. If nobody is sure who's going, both parents end up texting each other, and sometimes both show up.
  • Texting while driving. The person driving is the one who needs answers most, and the one who shouldn't be on the phone.

Ask for a Ride With Pick Me Up

With Cerqa, your teen doesn't have to send a string of texts. On the home screen, they tap Pick me up, choose who should pick them up, and tap Ask for a ride.

They can add a short note about where to find them, like "Front entrance by the flagpole", and choose whether they need the ride now, in 15 minutes or in 30 minutes.

The parent gets a simple request, like "Ana needs a ride". One tap on Go starts directions straight to them. If the parent can't make it, they tap Can't, and your teen knows to ask someone else.

Watch the Car Arrive

Once the parent is on the way, you both share your location until the pickup. Your teen can see the car moving on the map and how far away it is, so there's no need to ask "how much longer?"

And if they walk to a different spot, maybe from the front entrance to the gym or the side gate, the parent's directions follow them. Nobody has to explain where they moved.

When the car pulls in, your teen can see exactly where it is in the lot and walk straight to it.

Everyday Family Sharing

School isn't the only pickup. There's practice, after-school jobs, friends' houses and late-night rides home.

Cerqa lets your family share locations as a group, so everyone can see who's on the way and when they'll arrive. On the free plan, groups of up to 5 people can share for up to an hour at a time. With Cerqa Pro, you can share for up to 24 hours.

It works between iPhone and Android phones, so a parent with an iPhone and a teen with an Android phone see each other on the same map. Here's more about sharing your location between iPhone and Android.

Privacy Stays in Your Hands

Your location goes only to the people you choose, and sharing always has an end. Either of you can stop sharing with one tap at any time. Cerqa isn't watching your family around the clock. It's there for the moments you need to find each other.

Cerqa accounts are for ages 13 and up, so Pick me up is a great fit for teens with their own phones.

Tips for Smoother Pickups

  • Pick a regular meeting spot. A landmark like the flagpole or the gym doors is easier to find than "out front".
  • Use the note. A few words about where you're waiting save a lot of back and forth.
  • Ask a little early. If practice ends at 5:00, ask for a ride in 15 minutes instead of waiting until you walk out.
  • Let the driver drive. The parent only needs to tap Go. Directions and the map do the rest.

Pickups don't need a dozen texts. One tap to ask, one tap to go, and everyone knows where everyone is.
